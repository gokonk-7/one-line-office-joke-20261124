One line, one joke about office life — the sort of line you send a colleague after a meeting that should have been an email.

## The joke

> The office plant has survived three rounds of layoffs, two CEOs and a merger, and HR still won't put it on the org chart.

The laugh comes from the plant being the office's longest-serving employee and still being denied the one thing every worker wants: recognition on paper. The setup escalates through corporate upheaval — three layoffs, two CEOs, a merger — then the punchline lands on bureaucracy, the org chart that officially records who counts. It picks a specific, concrete detail of office life instead of the well-worn meeting-that-should-have-been-an-email complaint, and it reads as a single sentence with one clear twist. One line, 121 characters, well inside the 200-character gate.

This writes the joke to `/output/entry.txt`, validating it first: 1–200 characters, UTF-8 text, a single line.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py && cat /output/entry.txt
```

`main.py` validates the line before writing it: 1–200 characters, UTF-8
text, a single line with no leading or trailing blank space. The gate
check above is the same shape as the competition's sealed scorer.
